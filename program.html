Keynote

Disaggregated and Software-Centric Mobile Networking: Challenges and Opportunities

Mahesh Marina
University of Edinburgh

Abstract: Over the past decade, mobile networks have undergone a fundamental architectural transformation, moving from monolithic, hardware-centric systems towards disaggregated, software-centric architectures. This shift, exemplified by software-defined RAN, Open RAN and cloud-native mobile networking, promises greater programmability, flexibility, scalability and faster innovation. At the same time, it introduces new challenges in system design, performance, resource efficiency and realistic experimentation. Equally, the software-centric view enables new opportunities. In this talk, I will provide my perspective on the drivers and enablers of disaggregated and software-centric mobile networking, the challenges and opportunities arising from this architectural transformation, and promising directions going forward, drawing on our research spanning RAN programmability, cloud-native mobile core architectures and experimental platforms.

Bio: Mahesh Marina is a Professor (Personal Chair of Networked Systems) in the School of Informatics at the University of Edinburgh. His research interests span networked systems, with a particular focus on wireless and mobile networking, cloud-native mobile networks, and AI-driven networked systems. He has held visiting professor and researcher positions at Johns Hopkins University, ETH Zurich, and Ofcom (the UK’s telecoms regulator). Previously, he was a Turing Fellow at the Alan Turing Institute (the UK’s national institute for data science and AI) for five years (2018–23) and served as Director of the Institute for Computing Systems Architecture at the University of Edinburgh for four years (2018–22). Prior to joining Edinburgh, he spent two years as a postdoctoral researcher in the UCLA Computer Science Department after earning his PhD in Computer Science from the State University of New York at Stony Brook. He is a Fellow of the IEEE, Fellow of the Royal Society of Edinburgh, and an ACM Distinguished Member. For more information about his profile and research, please visit his website.
